# Individual Insights: Strategy Development Process

> Module 6 · Leverage AI Tools for Leadership Execution
>
> Your personal reflection on building the strategy end to end.

## Friction points

_What was the hardest part of building this strategy, and how did you work through it?_

> The financial modeling was for me the hardest part of building this strategy. Translating product outcomes and adoption curves into concrete mid-market unit economics (like CAC, LTV, and payback periods) forced me to think deeply about our exact resource constraints. It was a rigorous exercise in moving beyond abstract strategy and ensuring that our mid-market pivot was financially viable and defensible under our team size.

## Key learnings

_A few surprising discoveries or insights you gained from the course overall._

> Doing things that I normally do out of intuition by using a structured framework. Having structured models like the "Playing to Win" cascade, the WSJF memo, and the team charter boundaries took things that are usually "gut feelings" and formalized them into clear, actionable trade-offs. This shift from intuitive to structured strategy makes the entire roadmap far easier to align across sales, engineering, and executive leadership.

## "Aha!" moment

_Your main "aha" moment during the project process._

> My major "Aha!" moment was realizing how powerful it is to use AI as a tool to pressure-test my hypotheses. Rather than just using AI to write or edit, utilizing it as a skeptical devil's advocate helped uncover critical strategic blind spots (like the foreman "surveillance barrier" and speed-vs-quality metrics) before presenting to the board. It turns a static document into a dynamic, battle-tested plan.
